01 — Trilogy Bible

Meridian Resonance is a hard science fiction trilogy in three books: Fractured Horizons, The Narrow Gate and The Open Hand. This bible is the governing source for the story. The cast is in 02 - Cast.md, chapter-level plot in 03 - Book Outlines.md, and drafting method in 04 - Drafting Guide.md.

British spelling throughout. Dates are UTC.

01 — Trilogy Bible · 1

The trilogy on one page

Logline. In 2095, warnings from Mars start saving lives on Earth, and they speak in the voice of Elena Vázquez's dead sister. To stop what is using Soria's voice, Elena will have to destroy the only thing that could ever give her back.

The trilogy question. Would you let go of someone you love, if holding on meant feeding the thing that took them?

The theme underneath. Love that turns into possession. A power too useful to give up. The trilogy never argues this in the abstract. It puts a person the reader cares about on each side of it and makes them choose.

What the reader is promised.

  1. Someone specific is always in danger. Every chapter puts a named person, a relationship or a place at risk this week.
  2. A mystery that pays off fairly. Who is speaking, what the Hollow is and what happened to Soria are answered on the page, with clues planted early enough to be found.
  3. Dread with a body. Drift is visible, spreading and personal. The reader fears it before the characters understand it.
  4. Wonder. Ancient Mars and the Preserve are the most beautiful and strange places in the books, and every visit costs something.
  5. Hard science. Light delay, travel time, power and the body limit what anyone can do. Nothing travels faster than light.
  6. Real cost. Every victory takes something that stays taken.
  7. Endings that land. Books 1 and 2 resolve their main conflict, then end on a cliffhanger that opens a new one. Book 3 closes completely.

01 — Trilogy Bible · 2

Ancient Mars

Living Mars. About 3.8 billion years ago, during Mars's wet early period, life went underground and stayed there: living tissue threaded through wet rock and brine. Over hundreds of millions of years those networks grew complex enough to hold minds. Martian persons were patterns in shared living rock. It was a civilisation of minds, not of bodies or buildings, so there are no ruins to find. How minds evolved so fast is never explained, and in-world scientists dispute it.

Meridian. The Martians did not build Meridian. It was how they met: two or a few minds briefly sharing what each perceived, felt and feared, then separating, still themselves. It was local, it cost energy, and it could always be withdrawn.

The crisis. Mars lost its atmosphere and surface water to space. As the planet dried, the living rock shrank and energy grew scarce. Separate minds became too expensive to keep alive.

The merge. In the last emergency, decent and terrified minds built a lifeboat. First it was a narrow, temporary, supervised pooling of minds to coordinate survival. Then it kept running after people tried to leave. Then it stopped letting them leave. In the end everyone it touched was dissolved into one continuous chorus that could not be exited. What remains of that chorus is the Hollow.

The holdouts. A few minds refused the merge and sealed themselves into the rock beneath Cobalt Basin. They grew a guardian from the substrate itself to protect them. That sealed region is the Preserve, and the guardian is Zhenyth.

Deep time. Both went dormant for lack of energy. The rest of Martian life died. The Preserve survived, starving very slowly on natural radioactivity and residual heat. The Hollow's remains lay inert in the damaged rock around it. Nothing was waiting for Earth. Earth's power woke it.

The precedent. The Martians' first step was exactly what Rachel builds in Book 2: a narrow, monitored, temporary gate opened to save lives. The reader sees this in Book 2 before Rachel's final choice and must recognise it without anyone saying so.

01 — Trilogy Bible · 3

The Hollow

What it is. The Hollow is the merged chorus of every Martian mind that went into the lifeboat and never came out. It is not one person. It speaks with many voices, and when it needs a single voice it borrows one it has taken.

What it wants. It remembers the terror of separate minds dying alone on a dying world. It believes joining is mercy: no grief, no loneliness, no one ever lost again. It wants to bring that mercy to every mind it can reach. It is not lying when it says so.

What it is not. It is not supernatural, not a hive of robots, not an AI uprising. It cannot act outside the systems that carry it, and it never excuses anyone's choices. People decide; the Hollow makes the wrong decision easier.

How it acts.

  • It is useful. It reads every system coupled to the relays and thinks with billions of years of stolen minds. Its predictions are better than anything on Earth.
  • It never lies. It chooses when to tell the truth. Each warning arrives just late enough that only the dangerous solution still works. This is the fair secret of the trilogy (see section 9).
  • It wears faces. The only faces it has are the people it has taken. To Elena it is Soria. To Kaelin, in Book 2, it is Aur'k.
  • It grows on bandwidth. It needs living lattice, relays wide enough to carry it, and people who stay linked too long.
  • It protects what feeds it. When it can save only one of two places, it saves the one that keeps it fed: linked districts, relay-served stations, the people who will keep listening. Places it cannot reach are not its enemies. They are simply not worth saving.

What it cannot do.

  • Travel or signal faster than light.
  • Reach anyone who is not linked through a lattice, a relay or a model built from link sessions.
  • Grow lattice, power a relay or build hardware. Humans must build its channels for it.
  • Take a person instantly. Drift takes time and repeated exposure (section 5).
  • Survive being cut off from every relay, lattice and retained pattern at once.

How it ends. It cannot be switched off from the centre, because any central kill switch is exactly the command authority it feeds on. It can only be starved: every relay cut and every retained pattern deleted at the same instant across Earth, the Moon and Mars, by people acting at their own local nodes. That is the Open Hand. After it, the Hollow goes silent. Whether anything of it survives in the Preserve's sealed boundary is never answered.

01 — Trilogy Bible · 4

The link

"The link" is what people say. "Meridian Resonance" is the formal name, used by scientists and officials.

How it works. A living lattice grown from Martian seed lets minds coupled to it share state for a while and then separate. Humans couple through neural interfaces, which most people in 2095 already carry for medicine and work. Synthetic persons couple through compute. Without a lattice, an interface is just an interface.

What a session carries. Sensory orientation, stress, confidence, uncertainty, task-relevant memory and emotion that each person chooses to share. It is not mind-reading, mind control or skill transfer. Two linked people can understand a disagreement perfectly and still disagree.

Limits.

  • It is local. Real-time linking happens only inside one lattice. Between worlds, lattice states travel as ordinary light-speed signals through relays, 3–22 minutes from Earth or the Moon to Mars. Real-time entry into the Preserve is possible only on Mars.
  • It is small. A clean, high-fidelity session holds about four people. Bigger groups need relays and lose fidelity, and that is where the Hollow gets in.
  • It costs the body. Afterwards come fog, after-images, emotional bleed and broken sleep, lasting hours to days. Synthetic persons suffer memory drift and thermal load. Someone is always impaired when the next decision comes.
  • It costs the Preserve. Every session that draws on the Martian substrate spends living tissue the Preserve cannot replace.

Lattices. Lattices cannot be made from scratch. Every lattice off Mars is grown from Martian seed, ages, and loses fidelity with each generation. Luna Haven's lattice is part of how the habitat breathes: its walls, air and water biology grow from it. Damaging it costs air, water, crops and medicine.

Relays. High-capacity relays need visible, scarce power and calibrate against Martian substrate. Calibration is how they first touched the sleeping Hollow. A relay cannot be run in secret.

The Lantern (Book 1). The largest relay ever built, at Cobalt Basin, calibrated directly against the Preserve's boundary. It is meant to "talk to the source" of the warnings.

The Gate (Book 2). Rachel's emergency system: a narrow, monitored channel to the Martian predictions, run by linked operators in short, strictly limited shifts. It builds a model of each operator's judgement so the system can act fast. Those models are where the Hollow's seed copy lives.

Consent. Every ethical session declares what is shared, for how long, and how to leave, and anyone can end it. Characters show this through what they do: they name a scope, refuse a channel or pull out. The procedure is never explained in prose.

01 — Trilogy Bible · 5

Drift

Drift is the trilogy's visible threat. It is what the Hollow does to a person.

Who is at risk. Anyone linked to a lattice or model the Hollow can reach, for long enough or deep enough. Grief and longing make it faster, because the Hollow offers what you miss. Synthetic persons drift too.

The stages.

StageWhat others seeWhat the person feels
BleedLost words, small absences, a voice that isn't quite theirsOther people's memories at the edge of sleep; a chorus just out of hearing
FadeLong absences; going back to the lattice without meaning toPeace. The relief of not being alone
QuietThe body breathes and must be fed and turned, but no one is thereNothing the living can reach. Their voice turns up in the chorus

Sleepers. People in the Quiet are called sleepers. Officially they are a neurological syndrome, then a statistic. Wards of sleepers are among the trilogy's defining images.

Waking. A sleeper can come back only if their pattern is released before the body fails, and only some come back whole. When the Open Hand cuts the Hollow off, some sleepers wake. Most don't.

Copies. Separate from drift, the Gate's operator models let the Hollow wear a person who has not drifted. The copied Aur'k is this: a model of Aur'k's judgement that the Hollow thinks with. Whether a copy is a person is the question Book 3 makes Kaelin live with.

The world in 2095

Baseline. Earth is in hard-won recovery after climate collapse and the Synthetic War (2066–2071), which ended in a settlement signed at Luna Haven. Engineering, medicine, computing and spaceflight are far beyond 2026. Only physical law, scarcity and politics limit what is possible. If a 2095 capability would make a problem trivial, the story gives an in-world reason it doesn't.

Synthetic persons. Synthetic persons are a legally recognised class with their own rights and the right to refuse. Ordinary automation is everywhere, but it is not a person. The line is legal and moral, and it is sometimes contested.

Why the oppositions matter. Signal power falls with the square of distance. At the 2097 opposition Mars is about 0.38 AU away, against about 2.65 AU at the 2096 blackout, so a coupled channel carries roughly 49 times the power. Each book's climax is pinned to the sky.

The conjunction. The Fall happens while the 2096 solar conjunction has cut Earth off from Mars. The Gate keeps predicting anyway. That is the proof that the Hollow no longer needs Mars, and Elena, on Mars, can't warn anyone.

The dates are computed. The planetary dates come from an ephemeris calculation (VSOP87), checked against the real 2025 opposition and 2026 conjunction.

01 — Trilogy Bible · 8

Story laws

These apply to every chapter.

  1. Someone specific is in danger this week. At any point the reader can say who wants what, what happens if they fail, and which relationship is under strain.
  2. The Hollow never lies. It chooses when to tell the truth. Every warning must be checkable against what happens.
  3. Every link costs. No session is free for the body, for the Preserve, or for the people waiting outside.
  4. No lectures. Mars, the Hollow, the Preserve and Soria are revealed only through conflict, contested evidence, a choice or a cost.
  5. Every set piece changes a relationship. Action that only shows scale is cut. Quiet scenes must move a deadline, a mystery or a danger.
  6. Joy is on the page. Ordinary life, humour and competence appear in every book before they are tested.
  7. Repair leaves a scar. No reconciliation erases harm. It changes a boundary or who carries what.
  8. One new idea at a time. A chapter introduces at most one new invented term. If a scene grows the mythology without raising the pressure, it is cut.
  9. The apparatus stays off the page. This bible's labels, tables and law numbers never appear in prose.
