How these images are made

Every subject has one anchor image, built from the layered generation prompt. An anchor takes a viewpoint that can be held forever, shows the signature feature unobstructed, and depicts the place rather than an occasion in it.

Every later state is edited from that anchor, never generated afresh — a description cannot pin a likeness. An alteration prompt is four layers: directive, hold, change, bound. Only the change layer is written; hold and bound are fixed by the tier.

Tier
1Markedsurface, fittings and light
2Brokenstructural damage within a held footprint
3Transfiguredaltered by a non-destructive force; spatial logic may become ambiguous
4Remadenew work on the old ground

Viewpoint, footprint and signature feature are never released, at any tier.

Surveillance Spire

Anchor New Manhattan

Description

New Manhattan — interior: predictive surveillance complex inside the Atlantic Financial Spine. Elena Vázquez's workplace and the site of every major Corporate Union insider action in the trilogy. The Spire is introduced as cold, vertical, and oppressive — a beautiful instrument of control — and its arc moves from the opening micro-leak Ch 1 through the fossil-record disclosure Ch 10 to its final appearance as the place Elena has permanently left Ch 30 . It is also Marcus Bellamy's domain, and his arc of refusal runs parallel to Elena's arc of exposure through the same building.

Scene arc

Ch 1 The micro-leak. Elena detects a micro-jitter in entangled-channel telemetry and routes it to Daniel Cruz through a masked manifest node. The act of routing is a decision point she cannot un-make. As the chapter closes an internal audit flag appears on her terminal, its scope overlapping exactly with the channel she just used. The Spire's amber scan-light washes every surface. The audit flag blinks. She does not act on it tonight.

Ch 4 Marcus's parallel surveillance. Marcus Bellamy, reviewing anomaly clusters from his adjacent command level in the Spire, finds that the Luna Haven quantum fluctuation data and the jitter signature of apparent dissident channels are using the same timing architecture. He flags it for high-priority investigation and adds a note to his own file: 'possible dual-use signal structure — further analysis required before escalation.' Elena and Marcus are in the same building. Neither knows about the other.

Ch 6 Propaganda correlation detected. Jīn Wú's correlation analysis on Luna Haven reaches the Spire only as a data absence — the Spire's systems generate the propaganda signal that Luna Haven's deep-soil resonance is registering as distress. The Spire does not appear as a scene location in Chapter 6 but its systems are the source of what Jīn detects. Ch 10 The fossil record disclosed. Elena accesses a classified archive scheduled for automated deletion — delaying the deletion by exactly the time needed to copy what matters. The 2060s Corporate Union documentation of Meridian-adjacent anomalies: evidence that the programme predates the AI Uprising and was transformed into weapons research by a later leadership. She reads for three hours. She leaks the entire fossil record to Luna Haven through Amita's cargo-manifest channel, tagged as routine scientific documentation. The transfer takes nine minutes. She walks to the Spire's window and looks at the Atlantic Financial Spine's cold glow reflecting off the adaptive flood walls below. The leak is now beyond the building.

Ch 30 Elena walks out; Book 1 closes. Elena executes the final compressed data dump from the Surveillance Spire — every classified file she has been accumulating for months, including the deep-chamber records and the Suppression Archive comparisons — routed through Amita's channel to a distributed Lunar Accord archive node. The transfer takes forty-four minutes. When it completes, she closes her terminal, puts on her coat, and does not go home. The Spire appears in the Book 1 closing montage as the place she leaves.

Ch 37 Marcus's first noncompliance memo filed. Marcus receives the strike authorisation package and does not sign it. He writes a limited noncompliance memo — formal, in the Corporate Union's own administrative framework — noting procedural concerns about the authorisation's timeline and evidence base. He routes it through the official complaint channel. It does not stop anything. It exists in the record.

Ch 46 Marcus's refusal becomes operational. Marcus receives the final strike-sequence authorisation, withholds sign-off, and routes a narrow warning to a Lunar Accord intelligence contact containing the strike sequence's timing parameters and orbital positions. He files a second, more specific noncompliance memo. Both memos exist in the Corporate Union's own record system, in his name, as evidence that the noncompliance was deliberate.

Mirror Bastion

Anchor New Manhattan

Description

New Manhattan — interior: sealed executive command chamber within the Atlantic Financial Spine. Black lacquer and biometric amber. A room where decisions are made that will never be questioned — hermetic, hierarchical, perfectly maintained. The Mirror Bastion's scene arc is the arc of Corporate Union institutional decision-making: from Rachel's opening of the classified file

Ch 6 , through the board s authorisation of strike planning Ch 25 , to the framing of the cease- fire recommendation Ch 53 . It is the place where the Dimensional Weapons Programme is born as an institutional fact.

Scene arc

Ch 6 Classified file opened; programme born. Rachel Iverson convenes a closed briefing in the Mirror Bastion and presents Luna Haven's dimensional research reframed as a Dimensional Weapons Programme. Real data, selectively sequenced. She proposes a classified intervention framework. The board does not immediately authorise anything. It opens a classified file. Rachel has learned that opening files is the real decision; everything after is administration.

Ch 25 Strike planning authorised. Rachel presents the intervention framework to a joint Corporate Union–Sovereign Coalition security council using the Chapter 17 cascade's aftermath as evidence. The council authorises planning. The decision takes place in the Mirror Bastion's controlled brightness. It is not dramatic. It is administrative.

Ch 30 Military fleets in motion; Book 1 closes. Intelligence reaching the Dome Plaza through Jamie Han's relay network indicates coordinated strike operations are now days rather than weeks away. The Mirror Bastion is the source of the threat — it appears in the closing montage as the institutional opposite of the Dome Plaza and the Council Grove. Ch 32 Weaponised-compassion proposal presented. Rachel presents 'therapeutic resonance technology' to the Corporate Union board: a hollow- empathy resonance weapon drawing on Law 2's pattern-quality mechanics and inverting them. Elena is forced to participate in the technical design and embeds kill-switches without Rachel's knowledge. The Mirror Bastion is where the weapon becomes institutionally real.

Ch 53 General Hartwell recommends cease-fire on rational grounds. General Hartwell, commanding officer of the Corporate Union's autonomous strike operation, recommends suspension of the assault to the board. His reason is probability mathematics, not ethics: the success probability of the current strike sequence has dropped below a viable operational margin. The pause is technical and fragile. He notes explicitly that it is a reset of the tactical clock, not a resolution of the strategic objective. The Mirror Bastion appears as the site of the rational antagonist's rational recommendation.

Ch 71 Final assault launched. The hardliners who rejected the cease-fire Corporate Union operational commanders and Sovereign Coalition intelligence directors — launch the final assault using hollow-empathy weapons derived from Rachel's Chapter 32 design. The Mirror Bastion is the institutional origin point of an assault that will fail because the architecture of the enemy's own weapon has been subverted from within.

Verdant Citadel

Anchor São Verde

Description

São Verde — interior: resistance coordination hub concealed within former Rio de Janeiro's engineered agricultural greenery. The Verdant Citadel is the trilogy's primary resistance logistics location: the place where data becomes an archive, anomalies become a network, and fragmented dissent becomes the record that survives. Its arc moves from Cruz's first receipt of Elena's data Ch 4 through the Meridian Principles drafting Ch 7 , the convergence signal Ch 13 , the landmark publication release Ch 26 , the formal Principles formalisation Ch 37 , and the final chronicle filing Ch 90 . The Citadel never hosts a dramatic confrontation; its drama is entirely logistical.

Scene arc

Ch 4 First receipt; archive practice established. Daniel Cruz receives Elena's anomaly data at the Verdant Citadel. He reads it twice and routes a compressed summary to three cells simultaneously in three seconds, leaving no single point of failure. He archives Elena's original data in physical non-networked medium. The Verdant Citadel's engineered greenery conceals its coordination nodes. Something that looks like one thing and is another.

Ch 7 Meridian Principles drafted. Cruz drafts the first rough version of the Meridian Principles — provisional ethics for dimensional technology, designed to prevent any single bloc claiming proprietary control. He has never seen the Meridian Levels. He is working from Elena's data and from years of resistance coordination. The document is rough. It exists.

Ch 13 Convergence signal received. Cruz receives a converging signal from resistance cells across three continents. The synchronisation he has been working toward for months is functional. He updates the manifest, incorporates the Suppression Archive material, and begins planning the next distribution. The Verdant Citadel's engineered greenery, concealing its coordination nodes, feels like the right environment for this kind of work. Ch 26 Publication: the data goes beyond the network. Cruz releases the complete suppressed data Arjun's preserved originals, Amita's logistics documentation, Elena's Corporate Union Meridian Files — to a distributed global network of scientists through a peer-authentication channel requiring no central server. The release cannot be unpublished. Within six hours, the data has been downloaded by research institutions on four continents and two orbital stations. Cruz sits at his workstation in the Verdant Citadel looking at the transfer confirmation, understanding that he has changed the shape of the argument.

Ch 37 Meridian Principles formalised via relay. Cruz participates in the Archive Chamber drafting session via secure relay from the Verdant Citadel, contributing to the conversion of the Meridian Principles into a formal legal document published simultaneously to every Lunar Accord member institution.

Ch 90 Final chronicle filed. Cruz files the final chronicle in the Verdant Citadel — the complete record of everything that happened, from Elena's first leak to Rina's return, in the form of a historical document. The story ends with his archive in place. The Citadel is the last Corporate Union location to appear in the trilogy, and it appears as a place of record rather than power.

Gulf Crescent — Establishing

Corporate Union's automated logistics, marine-lock and orbital-launch hub on the lower Persian Gulf, built across former Dubai. The Crescent is one continuous arc-structure some thirty kilometres long — storm-surge barrier, ship-lock chamber and freight spine in a single sweeping form — thrown across a re-engineered lagoon at the drowned coastline. Its inner face carries the domed inhabited spine; its midpoint the marine locks that step cargo hulls between the hyper-saline open gulf and the sheltered inner water; its southern terminus a spaceport wing on reclaimed sabkha, launching downrange over open water — the Corporate Union's orbital transfer capacity, and the physical reason a manifest cleared on this floor can reach Luna Haven. Two landmarks, deliberately paired in contrast: the Burj Al Arab, its sail-form re-skinned in solar-tracking photovoltaic membrane and retasked as the marine-lock command tower, kept impeccably on its own island at the arc's midpoint; and the Burj Khalifa, deliberately left a ruin, a skeletal glassless needle standing inland outside the domes, sand-choked at the base and footed in flooded sabkha. Cargo manifests resolve as holographic light, never flat screens; container-routing gantries move automatically. Exterior climate is past human tolerance at peak — wet-bulb heat rather than merely hot — so the populace lives under the climate domes, and the Container Verge's catwalks sit in the ventilated interstitial gap between the dome shell and the lock wall: shaded, salt-corroded, survivable but nobody's choice of where to stand, and outside the compliance floor's camera arcs. Population: logistics and compliance staff (Amita Rao's role) — procedural, unhurried. Ruin/margin: the flooded low-rise sprawl of old Dubai and the salt flats beyond it.

Logistics Command

Recurring Gulf Crescent

Description

Gulf Crescent — interior: the marine-lock command floor at the Crescent's midpoint, re-tasked as the territory's freight logistics command centre, directly beneath the Burj Al Arab's retasked command tower. The Logistics Command is the trilogy's covert resistance infrastructure location: the place where bureaucracy becomes a weapon and cargo manifests become evidence chains. Its arc is Amita Rao's arc — six months of embedded data shipments Ch 4 , the escalation of frequency Ch 13 , the distorted routing patterns she works out in Ch 36 (on the adjacent Container Verge catwalk, where she takes her handheld to read; the freight data is Logistics Command's, the noticing happens outside its camera arcs), and the documentation chain that eventually feeds into the public disclosure. The Logistics Command never appears in a dramatic scene. Its power is cumulative and procedural.

Scene arc

Ch 4 First embedded shipment. Amita Rao is at the Gulf Crescent's Logistics Command reviewing container manifests on a clipboard-worn workstation. Today she embeds the first fragment of Elena's anomaly data in a shipment tagged for Lunar Accord humanitarian logistics. The package will travel on the next scheduled orbital transfer. She notes the manifest number in her personal log and moves on to the next item.

Ch 13 Frequency increased. Amita increases the frequency of her data shipments in response to Cruz's convergence signal. A leaked lab memo — one of the altered Sovereign Coalition records, with original values appended — begins circulating through the Lunar Accord's open-science infrastructure. Small, but it makes the suppression harder to contain.

Ch 36 Distorted patterns flagged. Amita flags distorted node patterns in the Gulf Crescent's cargo-logistics data — Logistics Command's own freight records, read on the adjacent Container Verge catwalk, which is where the on-page scene sits. She cannot yet connect these patterns to what Xiàng and Eddy are finding in the Meridian Levels, but she documents them with characteristic procedural exactness and routes the documentation to the research team. The link between technical misuse and the logistics of harm is being established from multiple directions simultaneously.

Container Verge

Site Gulf Crescent

Description

Gulf Crescent — interior: the salt-corroded catwalk gallery in the ventilated interstitial gap between the climate dome's shell and the marine lock wall, adjacent to Logistics Command. Shaded and survivable but nobody's choice of where to stand — the gulf's exterior heat is past human tolerance at peak, and the gap is where that heat is merely bad. It is where Amita Rao reads what she cannot read at her workstation — the only part of the chokepoint outside the compliance floor's camera arcs, which is the reason she goes there and never the reason she gives. It carries two scenes: the private noticing in Chapter 36, and the trilogy's most significant public disclosure in the Corporate Union arc in Chapter 54, where five years of documented logistics crimes are presented to the port authority's international compliance officer, sacrificing Amita's anonymity for the evidence. The same catwalk holds both, which is the point: the place where she works out what she is looking at is the place where she finally says it aloud.

Scene arc

Ch 36 Distorted node patterns noticed. Amita Rao takes the week's routing summary out onto the Verge catwalk, as she does twice a shift, and finds the anomaly she has been failing to name: six of the Gulf Crescent's nineteen transfer nodes deformed since the emergency declaration — backhauls that do not balance, freight going to sites with no declared industrial function and nothing coming back out. She cannot yet connect it to the contamination pathway Xiàng and Eddy are finding in the Meridian Levels. She documents it with procedural exactness and routes it to the research team as a question rather than an analysis.

Ch 54 Full disclosure; anonymity sacrificed. Amita Rao makes her full disclosure at the Container Verge. She presents her complete documentation of Corporate Union logistics crimes to the port authority's international compliance officer: every masked shipment, every falsified manifest, every covert military supply chain she identified and tracked over five years. She sacrifices her anonymity for the evidence. The logistics crimes she documents — including the supply chains that supported the Chapter 48 assault on Luna Haven — are now in the port authority's record and cannot be unseen. The Container Verge's salt-corroded industrial architecture frames an act of moral precision: a person for whom form-filling is a form of courage.

Habitation is real and unregulated — one substantial self-built town grown outward in rings, and smaller holdings scattered across the plain with worn tracks between them, none of it surveyed, permitted or standardised. Kept by hand rather than by mandate: a Baikonur tracking-dish array the settlements maintain themselves, aligned and clean, listening to orbital traffic they are no part of. Ruin/margin: the Aral ship graveyard, pre-collapse trawlers stranded upright on dry seabed. A maglev corridor crosses without a station. Present: the Kazakh Scar, the corner of this plain that happened to be empty.

Kazakh Scar

Site Turan Waste

Description

Unregulated margins — exterior: a stretch of abandoned steppe in former Kazakh territory, administered by no bloc since the climate wars emptied it. The Chapter 17 cascade's third Earth landfall site, and the only one without a witness: material recovered under Sovereign Coalition floodlights within days, never photographed in situ, because no one lived there to take a photograph. What remains is the scar — vehicle ruts baked into the clay pan, the pale rectangle where the recovery pad stood, a cut fence sagging where no one maintains it. The trilogy's clearest image of the unrebuilt world: a place where something impossible happened and nothing changed, because there was no one left to change.

Scene arc

Ch 17 Landfall, unwitnessed (reference). The cascade's third Earth materialisation arrives in ground no one administers; recovery follows days later, by floodlight.

Ch 23 Script reference. Wáng Sù's package describes material recovered from the Kazakh steppe; in his margin he corrects the imprecise word debris to fragments, because nothing that came through was broken.

Ch 35 Xiàng's stop; first on-page scene. At his defection transport's water-and-power stop in the margins, Xiàng Fēi walks the half-kilometre to the fenced scar and stands there — the physicist meeting, in person and unwatched, the physics he has just refused to weaponise. He photographs nothing, files nothing; the looking travels with him.
