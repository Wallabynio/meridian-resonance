Wardrobe

What each territory wears

Luna Haven

Wardrobe family

  • Name: Luna Haven everyday dress.
  • Who wears it: everyone who lives on Luna Haven: Moon-born, arrivals, synthetic citizens, visitors who stay.
  • Territory / bloc: Luna Haven, Lunar Accord.

Core wardrobe brief

  • One-sentence brief: a colony that dresses in its own biology: grown, fitted, quietly lit, and worn the way any people dress for the place that made them.
  • Primary impression: ease and belonging; competence without uniformity.
  • Social function: local identity; legibility in low light, from the piping; privacy (see diagnostics below).
  • Why it exists in-world: every interior is pressurised and breathable, so the suit is chosen, not demanded: lifestyle dress, never survival gear.
  • Scenes that most need it: Elena walked into a district vault (Book 2); the Dome Plaza sessions (Ch 77, 90); the siege; arrivals after the Ch 35 influx; grief after Ch 86.

The variation the colony needs

One garment for five million people is the gap. Variation runs along these axes, all inside the suit's rules:

AxisHow it shows
Membrane colourA muted living range: undyed pale membrane, basalt and regolith greys, lichen and moss greens, deep teal-blacks. The colour comes from what the membrane is grown with, never from a printed pattern
CutClose-fitted to loose; long or short sleeve; high or open collar; panelled for work or plain for civic hours
WearMembranes pale and soften at the knees, cuffs and shoulders with age; a long-worn suit is visibly comfortable
WorkReinforced panels, tool loops and stained gloves on the crews (below)
Moon-born or arrivalMoon-born wear the suit as second nature, often open at the collar; an arrival's first suit is new and stiff, given with First Breath, and their Earth clothes are kept for home
OccasionOverlayer for formal hours; piping brightened for ceremony; dimmed for grief
HouseholdA wall cutting carried, a small light at the belt

Context coverage

Civic / everyday

  • Core silhouette: the bio-tech suit, sleek and fitted, with breathable membrane panels, integrated diagnostics and bioluminescent edge piping at the seams.
  • Typical garments: the suit. Over it, at need, the light grown overlayer.
  • Footwear: soft-soled grown boots, flexible, made for walking in one-sixth gravity and quiet on living floors.
  • Accessories: a small light, and a cutting from the household's wall. Forearm diagnostics where the suit carries them; several Look demands already place them there.
  • Everyday palette: the membrane range above; the piping's low teal is the only light on the body.

Professional / labour

  • Membrane and bulkhead crews: abrasion panels at the knees and forearms, a tool loop at the hip, gloves worn to the substrate's stain. Maintenance crews hold the highest everyday standing and stop authority. That standing shows as wear, never as insignia.
  • Wall-tenders: gloves and sleeves stained by the mycorrhizal substrate; piping often dimmed by habit, as the Look demands already say of Habitat Corridors workers.
  • Research staff (Meridian Levels, Archive): the suit, with sleeves closed and the collar high in the cold strata.
  • Security: the suit "cut for civic rather than militarised work". No armour, no visor.
  • Radiator and surface crews: outside the pressure envelope, and only there, a true pressurised EVA suit: sealed helmet, life support, thermal protection, visually unmistakable from the bio-tech suit. Synthetic crews wear the surface suit instead.
  • Rank markers: none by rank (Accord).

Formal / ceremonial

  • Ceremony: the suit with its piping brightened. Over it, the light overlayer: grown, long, falling straight from the shoulder, in a deeper tone of the wearer's membrane.
  • A wedding in the lunar night: brightened piping is an extravagance of the body's own light, when the colony's margin is narrowest.

Domestic / intimate

  • At home: the suit open over its under-layer.
  • Arrivals at home: their Earth garments, kept and worn there or on days that matter, which read as where someone came from.
  • Intimacy: intimates open their suits' diagnostics to each other, the one reading the colony never shares.
  • Grief: the mourner's piping turned down for a lunar cycle.

Climate / field / travel

  • Colder districts and strata: the overlayer.
  • The tether passage: arrivals travel in Earth clothes and are given their first suit at First Breath.
  • The open surface: the EVA suit only, and a surface scene reads as exceptional.

Materials and construction

  • Membrane: grown from the same bio-integrated materials as the colony; breathable; colour from its growth medium.
  • Piping: bioluminescent and biological, honestly dim, never a lamp; the colony's teal.
  • Diagnostics: report to the wearer only. The colony's walls sense a body to light the way and are forbidden to know who it is, and the suit keeps the same rule.
  • Fastening: grown seams that close by pressure.
  • How it ages: pales and softens at the joints.
  • How it is repaired: re-grown, not stitched. The Earth register shows its repairs; Luna Haven's membrane heals its own, and a re-grown patch reads only as a slightly fresher shade. The two registers differ here as they do in light.

Silhouette and styling rules

  • Always: sleek and fitted; piping at the seams; the membrane's living colours; worn as clothing, open at the collar when at ease
  • Never: helmets, seals or anything reading as EVA indoors; a superhero catsuit sheen; the whole garment glowing; neon colours; armour plates; logos or bloc insignia; present-day sportswear

Further notes

  • Children's suits are re-grown at each stage of growth; outgrown membrane goes back to the walls.
  • Intimates open their suits' diagnostics to each other, the one reading the colony never shares.
Prompt clauses
  • Everyday: "A fitted Luna Haven bio-tech suit of grown membrane in [one colour from the living range], soft and worn at the joints, with a thin low teal bioluminescent piping along the seams; soft-soled grown boots; no helmet, no seals, nothing of a spacesuit."
  • Formal: "…under a long grown overlayer falling straight from the shoulder, piping brightened."
  • Grief: "…the piping turned down almost to nothing."
  • Avoid: "No spacesuit, helmet or visor; no glowing garment; no neon; no armour; no logo; no present-day sportswear."
Lunar Accord, Earth territories

Wardrobe family

  • Name: Accord Earth eco-tailoring.
  • Who wears it: the residents of the three Earth territories. Synthetic citizens wear the same tailoring, adapted; arrivals wear it as they settle.

Core wardrobe brief

  • One-sentence brief: clothes made to be kept: cut so nothing is wasted, mended so the mending shows, and worn by people who intend to stay.
  • Primary impression: warmth, craft, unhurried dignity.
  • Social function: provenance carried on the body (the etched coordinates); repair as a value, not a shortage; class shown only in the finish
  • The one absolute: no emissive element of any kind, no piping, no integrated light. It is a rule, not a tendency, and it is the whole contrast with Luna Haven.

Shared grammar (all three territories)

ElementRule
ConstructionZero-wastage heritage tailoring: interlocking geometric panels that consume the whole cloth
FibreReclaimed high-end fibre: bio-cellulose, recovered ocean polymer, micro-woven hemp; linen and denim among them
CutStructured European drape and asymmetric lapels married to relaxed, fluid, oversized silhouettes: duster coats, wide-brimmed solar shields
DetailWorked into the material, never added on; pre-collapse map coordinates laser-etched fine along the seams
The markEvery garment carries at least one mark of provenance or repair, made to a couture standard; etched coordinates count as one
PaletteOchre, sun-bleached sage, terracotta, raw clay, linen, denim, with restored warm gold accents
LightNone. Daylight on craft is the whole effect

Context coverage

Civic / everyday

  • Core silhouette: clean, well-fitted panel-cut garments; the duster or long coat; a solar shield outdoors.
  • Typical garments: civic clothing in hemp canvas or linen; the civic overlayer (Elowen's is the canon example).
  • Footwear: grown or resoled leather-substitute shoes and boots, resoled visibly.
  • Accessories: a notebook and a mended bag.
  • Everyday palette: as the shared grammar.

Professional / labour

  • Repair rosters and master repairers: aprons and sleeve guards in heavy hemp; tools carried in sewn loops. A master repairer's own garments carry the finest marks in the district, the trade's quiet signature.
  • Roster-keepers and heat-call staff: shade-cut layers and a wide solar shield, worn long hours; repairs quick and sturdy.
  • Field monitors and terrace workers: Earth-side field clothing, shade-cut, in sandstone and terracotta (Thandi's demand is the canon example).
  • Civic and processing staff (the Sydney Hub): eco-tailored hemp-canvas civic clothing, matte, mended visibly at cuff and hem (Priya's demand).
  • Rank markers: none (Accord).

Formal / ceremonial

  • Ceremony: the best mended garment a person owns. Formal coats keep structured European drape over a visibly ordered panel cut (Alexandre's demand).
  • High-status materials: the same fibres. Ceremony shows in the finish and the age of the mending, never in new cloth.

Domestic / intimate

  • At home: old, soft, mended things.
  • Intimacy: intimates mend each other's clothes.
  • Gifts: a mended thing, mended by the giver.
  • Grief: one tear left unmended for a year in a garment the mourner wears daily — the repair ethic inverted.

Climate / field / travel

  • Heat season: the widest solar shields; shade-cut layers; the duster worn open. On heat-call nights, people arrive at the cool halls in whatever they were wearing, and nobody is turned away.
  • Travel: travel clothing in the ochre-and-terracotta palette, mended with visible, careful stitching (Ines's demand).

Class, quietly

  • The time-rich: marks finished to couture standard, repairs that have been redone and refined over years.
  • The busy poor, who carry the rosters and cannot afford slowness: repairs that are honest, quick and sturdier, made to last a season of work. Never shabby, never patched carelessly.

The difference is visible only up close, to people who know cloth. It is the Accord's hidden inequality made physical, and the prose may notice it.

Arrivals

People who cross into the Accord at the Sydney Hub arrive in their own bloc's clothes. Their first Accord garment is given by the district as a gift, mended by the giver (the gift custom). They may go on wearing their own clothes as long as they like. Lǐ Jūn's later linen and denim "with skilfully stitched visible repair" is the canon example of a settled arrival.

Materials and construction

  • Fibres: as the shared grammar.
  • Fastening: sewn and buttoned; buttons often recovered.
  • How it ages: fades in sun along the shoulders and shield brims; wears soft.
  • How it is repaired: visibly, as craft: stitching, patchwork, darning in a contrasting but palette-true thread; every repair excellent; nothing hidden; the contrast with Luna Haven, which re-grows its membrane

Silhouette and styling rules

  • Always: panel-cut and fitted; one couture-standard mark; the palette; daylight
  • Never: anything emissive; shabbiness or rags, which read as poverty rather than craft; logos; present-day sportswear or fast fashion; the same "mended at one…" placement on every character

Synthetic citizens

  • the same tailoring, with seams following the body's
  • fine gloves over the reading fingertips
  • the column overlayer for ceremony
  • the glove left off for a day in grief

Further notes

  • Children's clothing is cut down and re-pieced from household garments; a child's first etched coordinate comes when they choose a place as their own.
Prompt clauses
  • Everyday: "Accord Earth eco-tailoring: a panel-cut [garment] in [fibre] in [palette colour], [regional accent], pre-collapse coordinates laser-etched fine along one seam, and one repair worked in visible, skilful stitching; a wide-brimmed solar shield; lit only by daylight."
  • Avoid: "No glowing seams or piping, no integrated light, no rags, no logos, no present-day sportswear."
Corporate Union

Core wardrobe brief

  • One-sentence brief: clothes as a service: beautifully made, leased by the season, and scored on the body.
  • Primary impression: polish, competence, the comfort of being looked after, and the faint cold of being looked at.
  • Social function: coverage made visible; the corporate optic; dress is scored.

Shared grammar

ElementRule
OwnershipMost clothing is leased by subscription, refreshed each season and returned. Owned clothes are either the premium "off-profile" choice of the very rich, or the uncovered's
CutTech-wear and lab coats; executive tailoring in black lacquer tones; sculptural, deliberately varied silhouettes at the top, as the bloc's towers are
PaletteBlack and anthracite, with biometric amber as the single accent
SurfaceFine technical fabrics; mirrored and lacquered finishes; seams bonded rather than sewn
The scoringGarments read their wearer's movements and report to the subscription, which feeds the score. A small amber tell at the collar or cuff shows the garment is live
VisorsMirrored visors on security and on executive floors: a sign of being watched-for. Ordinary people rarely wear them
WritingBrand marks are the Union's in-world writing: small, exact, on the garment's tab or lining, never a slogan

Context coverage

Civic / everyday

  • Covered citizens: this season's subscription in black and anthracite, amber at the tell; tech-wear for most work, lab coats in research (Ines's institutional researchwear).
  • Carried: a licensed, scored handheld, and a contraband notebook if they are brave.

Professional / labour

  • Security: an anthracite-and-black uniform, rank markers in amber as its only ornament (Marcus's demand); mirrored visors on duty. Facilities security in plain anthracite with a small amber shoulder tab (Viktor's demand).
  • Executive floors: tailoring in black lacquer tones (Rachel's demand).
  • Military: unadorned, functional military tailoring (Hartwell's demand).
  • Workers on terraces and docks: hard-wearing subscription workwear, cleaned and swapped by the service. Daniel's hard-worn civilian work clothing is the edge of the system.

Formal / ceremonial

  • Ceremony: brand-new and scored. A new season's release is itself a social occasion.

Domestic / intimate

  • Home: branded comfort.
  • Gifts: a subscription, which Accord people take as an insult.
  • Grief: a muted badge, and the household's score frozen for a quarter.

The uncovered

  • Owned garments outside any subscription, kept going for years and repaired by hand; no amber anywhere, because nothing reports.
  • In the Union it reads as poverty. To an Accord eye it reads as something close to freedom.

Synthetic persons in the Union

Synthetic persons in the Union are licensed devices in law. What they wear is the licensee's choice and usually a livery. That is the contrast the Accord's rule, "dress is chosen, never a livery", is written against.

Silhouette and styling rules

  • Always: a single amber accent; impeccable finish; variety and luxury at the top.
  • Never: repetitive anonymous uniforms on civilians; present-day logos; the Accord's visible mending; Luna Haven's grown membrane; glowing garments: the amber is a small tell, not a light source
Prompt clauses

  • Avoid: "No present-day logos, no sportswear, no visible mending, no glowing garment."
Sovereign Coalition

Core wardrobe brief

  • One-sentence brief: clothes issued by the state: crisp, correct, legible at a glance, and cut to say where you stand.
  • Primary impression: order, discipline, a cold elegance.
  • Social function: credential class worn on the body; dress is issued.

Shared grammar

ElementRule
IssueEveryday clothes come from the work unit, by credential class. Private garments are rare and kept out of sight
CutInstitutional uniforms, high collars, military tailoring
ClassShown in the collar and the tailoring: the higher the class, the higher and more structured the collar and the finer the cut
PalettePolished grey composite, with cold state-blue piping as the colour of the trim (a colour, not a light)
HonoursWorn on the uniform for ceremony; refusing an honour is taboo
WritingState writing is official: credential numbers, unit marks and honours in Mandarin, Cantonese in the Pearl Delta and Russian at Baikal, small and exact

Context coverage

Civic / everyday

  • Issued basics in grey composite, trimmed in state blue (Lǐ Jūn's grey composite basics; Arjun's muted, unmarked civilian clothes).
  • Carried: a credential and a state handheld.

Professional / labour

  • Officials and ministers: immaculate, dark, precise tailoring cut to read as authority on camera, with a high structured collar (Wáng Sù's demand).
  • Military: institutional grey military tailoring, high-collared (Tán Měi's demand).
  • Research: grey composite dress under an insulated lab coat, state-blue piping at collar and cuffs (Xiàng's demand).
  • Workers: issue by work unit, as in the territory accents.

Formal / ceremonial

  • Ceremony: uniform with honours. Emergency Day's state ceremony is the year's great uniformed occasion, and after the renunciation its meaning is contested.

Domestic / intimate

  • Home: issued wear. A grandparent's pre-war object, hidden, is the household's heirloom. A private garment, kept at home, is the same kind of quiet act.
  • Grief: a white band with the credential number.

Synthetic persons

Autonomous synthetic minds are prohibited in the Coalition, and the renunciation leaves the prohibition standing. There is no synthetic wardrobe here.

Silhouette and styling rules

  • Always: crisp; high-collared; grey and state blue; legible class.
  • Never: Union amber or lacquer; visible mending; glowing trim: the blue is cloth, not light; present-day military camouflage
Prompt clauses
  • Everyday: "Sovereign Coalition issued dress in polished grey composite, crisply tailored, a high structured collar, cold state-blue piping at the collar and cuffs."
  • Avoid: "No amber, no visible mending, no camouflage, no glowing trim."
Neo Singapore and the Unregulated Margins

1. Neo Singapore

Core brief

  • One sentence: legible as belonging somewhere, unreadable as belonging fully anywhere.
  • Social function: the patch — belonging or neutrality, worn or withheld.

Grammar

ElementRule
GarmentsMixed-bloc civilian transit wear; layered garments in grey, stone and linen
The patchEach garment can carry one bloc's accent patch without visual conflict. You do not ask anyone's bloc; a patch is worn and its absence is respected
Tri-colour lightAmber, blue and gold boundary light is in the environment, never worn
Treaty staffPlain stone-grey and linen, never a patch: the one people in the city who may not show a bloc
GriefA plain grey patch
ObjectsA neutral bag with patches of every bloc; treaty passes framed at home

Variation

  • Transit travellers: the most layered, dressed for two climates at once.
  • Residents: the plainest, with linen worn soft.
  • The patchless: by choice or by loss. A missing patch can read as defection before anyone has defected (the Xiàng seed).

Never

  • One bloc's full grammar.
  • Tri-colour light on the body.
  • Present-day airport clothing: the period test of the Transit Lounge.

2. The Unregulated Margins

Core brief

  • One sentence: clothes made by hand from what the walkers found, worn by people who chose to leave every counter.
  • Social function: exit, freedom from every counter. Hospitality: water is offered before any question.

Grammar

ElementRule
MakingHand-made; each piece its own material, as each wall in Turan Waste is its own material
SalvageBloc garments salvaged and re-cut until no bloc reads in them; Union subscription cloth loses its tell; Coalition grey is re-dyed or patched over
ClimateLayered for hard sun, salt wind and cold nights: head wraps, long sleeves, a heavy outer layer
PaletteThe settlement's own hand-worked register: pale salt clay, sun-bleached timber tones, undyed fibre, faded salvage colour
RepairVisible everywhere and better than what it replaced (Turan Waste's own rule for its buildings, carried to cloth)
ObjectsA water vessel and tools, carried and handed down
GriefAsh on cloth
LightUnmediated daylight; after dusk, the hand-made dark of filament, cook-fire and lamp. Nothing on the body is lit

Variation

  • Walkers: lightest and most layered, for travel.
  • Settled households: heavier work clothes; aprons and gloves at the waterworks and the dish array.

Never

  • A bloc's recognisable uniform worn whole.
  • Rags or squalor: the Margins are inhabited by choice, not abandoned.
  • Anything emissive.
  • Present-day clothing worn as found.
Prompt clauses
  • Neo Singapore: "Layered transit wear in neutral grey, stone and linen, one small bloc accent patch [or none]; tri-colour boundary light in the environment only."
  • Margins: "Hand-made layered clothing, each piece a different material, salvaged cloth re-cut so no bloc reads, head wrap against hard sun, visibly and skilfully mended; lit only by open daylight."
