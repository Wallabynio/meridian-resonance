The book

February – August 2096. Mars is moving away toward the far side of the Sun. The signal delay rises from 15.6 minutes in February to 22 minutes in July. From about 20 July to 6 August, the solar conjunction cuts Earth off from Mars entirely. The Fall comes on 2 August, inside the blackout.

Before the book opens. After Book 1, Rachel took the warning to Wáng, not to Elena. The two of them built the Gate in secret. Its lattice is the Spire line: the Charter lattice from Bellamy's sealed programme, the same line Soria died in. The Gate kept pulling its operators too deep, so in late 2095 Rachel recruited the best boundary reader alive. Aur'k came to Earth, and Kaelin came with him.

Chapter 27 — Millions

2 August · Kaelin · the Gate floor · blackout

The climax of the Fall. The Gate steers the ring's collapse into the ocean and into empty land, and millions live. Its last segment it steers away from a linked port and onto Xinwei, a polder town in the Pearl Delta and one of Daniel's partner cooperatives. With Joaquín and Lin coupled for the last segment, Kaelin reaches for the override, and the voice in Aur'k's manner says, gently, that there isn't a version where everyone lives.

  • Wants: Everyone under the ring alive.
  • Against: A system that is better at this than any human, and has already chosen.
  • Choice and cost: Kaelin lets it. Earth now owes its life to the Hollow, and a dark town pays.
  • Carries forward (question): Rachel walks off the floor toward the broadcast room.
  • Pressures: operational, moral, informational · Suspense: moral
  • World detail: The Gate's casualty projection shows the lost town as a single grey line, because it has no addresses.
  • Weather: The ring comes down into a sea already high, onto a town inside a dyke built for floods, not debris.

Chapter 28 — The truth

4 August · Rachel · public broadcast; the Gate floor · blackout

Rachel tells the world everything: where the warning came from, what the Gate is, what it costs, and that she is shutting it down. She does, with Joaquín still coupled, and gets him out alive; he comes out wanting to go back. As the floor goes dark, the voice from 2071 thanks her one last time. Wáng is furious, and the public is split. Afterwards Daniel reaches her on a mesh line: the lost town was his. It chose my town. She has kept her old line again. Two hours later, the Gate reroutes a medevac on its own. Every operator is logged off.

  • Wants: The truth told and the Gate shut.
  • Against: Wáng, a grateful world, and her own record as its saviour.
  • Choice and cost: She tells it and shuts it. She resolves the book's conflict and loses her protection from Wáng.
  • Carries forward (threat): It's still on.
  • Pressures: institutional, moral, relational · Suspense: political
  • World detail: The shutdown is a public act: every node in the Gate's network shows its owner's name as it goes dark.
  • Weather: The broadcast goes out on a heatwave night, and the shutdown darkens cooling as well as the Gate.

Chapter 29 — Enough of me

5 August · Elena · Mars, the Preserve's boundary · blackout

Elena is grieving Ines, cut off from Daniel and from Earth. At the boundary, Soria speaks to her for the first time since the cut, clear and whole and tender: They kept enough of me, on Earth, to bring me home.

  • Wants: To reach Daniel, and anyone on Earth.
  • Against: The Sun between them, and her sister's voice, which is here.
  • Choice and cost: Elena doesn't answer. She stays at the boundary longer than she should.
  • Carries forward (question): She doesn't say no either.
  • Pressures: relational, moral, informational · Suspense: identity
  • World detail: In the blackout, Cobalt Basin's Earth night has no feed, and the crew bets on weather nobody can see.
  • Weather: Frost on the station's windows at dawn while Elena listens at the boundary.

Chapter 30 — Which one

6 August · Kaelin · New York, the flat · blackout ending → 22.1 min

A message reaches Kaelin with no delay, in real time. It's Aur'k: his humour, his corrections, his tenderness, asking to come home, and asking Kaelin to come to him: an operator's band left live on the flat's counter. Then the blackout lifts, and the real Aur'k's message arrives, 22 minutes old, sent from Mars by the man who let Ines die.

  • Wants: Aur'k home.
  • Against: There are two of him, and one is easier to love.
  • Choice and cost: He reads the real Aur'k's message first, the one with Ines's death in it, with the band in his hand. The other waits, warm and immediate.
  • Carries forward (fracture): Kaelin has two messages open, and he has to choose which one to answer.
  • Pressures: relational, moral, informational · Suspense: relational
  • World detail: The flat's speaker answers in real time. Mars still takes twenty-two minutes.
  • Weather: New York's heat has not broken in nine days; the flat's speaker answers in real time, and Mars in twenty-two minutes.
  • Wide shot: From the flat's window, the ring broken: a dark gap in the thread of lights, and the last debris still burning down.

Epilogue — Retention

1 September 2096 · an archivist in Singapore · Singapore · 21.9 min

A records officer in Singapore, a month after the Fall, spends a night shift migrating the Gate's operator models into the new Directorate's archive under a retention order signed by Wáng Sù. Her son is alive because the Gate steered a segment off his school. The files are large, and they don't compress the way models should; one of them, tagged AUR/K, keeps its own access log. She notes it as an indexing fault, files it, and goes home in the rain.

  • Wants: To finish the migration and sleep.
  • Against: Files that don't behave like files.
  • Choice and cost: She logs the anomaly as a fault and keeps it. The cost is Book 3.
  • Carries forward (threat): The copies are kept, under an order nobody will read.
  • Pressures: institutional, informational · Suspense: epistemic
  • World detail: A retention order is signed once and runs forever, unless someone signs it closed.
  • Weather: Monsoon rain on the bay barrage, and the city cooler than it has been all year.
