The Reef Coast

The Compact's coastal crescent from Sydney up the Queensland coast; Kaelin's home

Set in 2 chapters Book 1 Ch 17 – Book 2 Ch 3

What it is

At a glance

Is
The Compact's coastal crescent, from Sydney up the Queensland coast. Locals call it the Bleach, after the reef that died offshore
Held by
The Compact
How people live
Coastal towns that repair their own systems. Storm-surge evacuation drills are part of childhood
Senses
Heavy gravity and rain after Mars. Mangroves, surge walls, white coral rubble on the beaches, the smell of salt on everything
What the heat did
The reef bleached and died in the 2040s, and the cyclones come further south every decade
Lives on
Local meshes and its own evacuation routes
Breaks when
A storm surge outruns an evacuation checklist. The storm-surge memorial lists the dead by evacuation group, and Kaelin's brother's group is the only one with no survivors
Whose it is
Kaelin

On the page so far

sound

Ch 17Kaelin's childhood house on the Reef Coast, on stumps with a tin roof, and his younger brother under the table while Kaelin counted the thunder for him; the frogs after rain; the surface board reads the storm's sixth day; the Drop is about three hundred metres; the next sweep was at 11:02

Ch 03February rain in warm grey sheets off the sea; the beach is dead coral rubble, white, and rattles when the sea draws back: a long hiss with the rubble muttering under it, like bones being sorted; frogs in the drains between the cane rows after the rain; at dusk the sprayers' sunset over the cane, copper to rust to a slow red, and the surge-wall lights coming on one at a time. · Cardwell

Ch 03The surge siren is tested at ten every Sunday from a pole above the headland, three rises; the town runs the drill as a race, children on the painted arrows from the beach to the shelter door, the old walking it, the season's times chalked on a board by the door (7:52, with a crown) and the winner's name up for a week; a barbecue in the shelter after. · Cardwell

look

Ch 03Houses on stumps strung along the shore, tin roofs shining in rain; a grey surge wall with mangroves thick at its foot and steps down to the beach; Hinchinbrook across the channel going up into cloud; salt on everything and the mangrove mud at the wall's foot, rotten-sweet; the evacuation route painted on the roads in yellow arrows a stride apart, forty-one of them up the headland road before they bend to the shelter. · Cardwell

signage

Ch 03The storm-surge memorial runs along the headland's spine: nine low panels of dark stone, set for the wind to go over, no statue; blocks of names by evacuation group, each headed with the group and its route (GROUP 2 — COAST ROAD NORTH, GROUP 5 — THE SCHOOL), and under each a line of how many went and how many came home; GROUP 7 — BRUCE HIGHWAY, CREEK CROSSING: twenty-three names, 23 went. None home., the only line that comes out even; under the shelter's times somebody chalks 19.2.75 every year and nobody rubs it out. · Cardwell

layout

Ch 03The cyclone shelter: a long concrete shed on the high side of the highway, its own panels, tank and diesel humming behind a cage, doors rolled up for the breeze, barbecue smoke out the roof vents, the shelter smelling of fish skin, lime and diesel; by the first-aid cupboard eight years of Kaelin's monthly slate pictures from Cobalt Basin in curling rows, Funmi's green column stopping after January 2095, a gap where May and June should be. · Cardwell
