    Photorealistic film still, 16:9 cinematic landscape, wide aerial establishing angle from about eight hundred metres looking obliquely down the length of the Gulf Crescent's arc in hard mid-morning light, deep depth of field, natural film grain, grounded documentary photography — not concept art, not illustration. The year is 2087, six decades past global climate collapse and the AI uprising, inside a civilisation deliberately rebuilt since. Composition and geometry first: one continuous arc, thirty kilometres long, sweeps unbroken from the left edge of the frame to the right across a re-engineered lagoon on the lower Persian Gulf — storm-surge barrier, ship-lock chamber and freight spine in a single form, visibly descended from real surge-barrier and sea-lock engineering. At its midpoint sit the marine locks: three parallel chambers stepped in line, each closed by a pair of tall mitre gates, two cargo hulls held inside them. Both sides of the barrier differ plainly — outside it the open gulf is hyper-saline, choppy and glaring white, and the outer water sits higher than the inner, breaking against the seaward face; inside it the lagoon is lower, sheltered, still and dark green, with a visible step in water level at the gates. On its own small island at the midpoint, unmistakably itself, stands the Burj Al Arab: a single sail-shaped tower, its front face bowed out in one great convex curve like a filled spinnaker, a steel exoskeleton mast rising the full height behind it to a fine point, one cantilevered disc platform projecting from the side near the top, one slender causeway joining the island to the arc — restored to gleaming condition, retasked as the marine-lock command tower. Along the arc's inner face runs a chain of seven domes, each a segmented shell of structural glazing bands resting directly on the spine's deck, and beneath every one of them a dense, multi-storey inhabited city spine of blocks eight to twenty storeys tall packed wall to wall — a working metropolitan district of roughly 600,000 people at real city scale, never a commune, never a compound, never a single dome standing alone in sand. Along the spine's crest a maglev guideway runs on paired piers with one maglev car on it, its skirt wrapping the beam so the car plainly rides the guideway rather than hovering. At the southern terminus a spaceport wing spreads across reclaimed sabkha flats, four launch gantries standing in a row, oriented downrange over open water. The frame holds two things at once, unsoftened toward each other: a structure whose whole purpose is throughput, and the drowned city it was built across. The upper third of the frame is that drowned sprawl — flooded low-rise blocks of old Dubai stretching inland under hyper-saline water and blown salt, streets gone to channels — and standing in it, genuinely derelict, the Burj Khalifa: a skeletal glassless needle, three setback tiers stepping inward, cladding long gone, spire snapped short, sand-choked at the base and footed in flooded sabkha. Across that sprawl: no cars, no vans, no trucks, no buses, no motorcycles, no wheeled vehicle of any kind, parked or moving; on the water inside or outside the barrier, no present-day boats, ferries, tugs, yachts or outboard motors. Held in the Villeneuve/Cuarón/Garland register: throughput as the whole point, the drowned city never hidden.

Prompt ordering discipline — position is priority

How Nano Banana Pro weighs different parts of a prompt, and what this project's prompt architecture should do about it. Full reasoning and worked step-lists live in Image Canon.md's Rules 8 and 9 (character branch). This entry is the quick-glance summary — read those two for the complete, authoritative text. Character-anchor reorder and the Ⓓ-echo refinement are now applied to the live characters.json/locations.json — see the applied-log entry below for the full scope.

The evidence
Primacy + recency + "lost in the middle"
Locations & territories
No change — already correct
Characters
Reordered and APPLIED — subject identity now leads
Alterations
Ⓓ-echo applied to 12 alterations
Why these settings

The evidence — Three independent sources agree: (1) general LLM/transformer position bias — early tokens and end-of-prompt tokens are both weighted more heavily than the middle; (2) Google's own published prompting guidance for this model family — lead with Subject, technical camera detail goes last; (3) this project's own round-2 African Belt test — putting material language before infrastructure produced a floating, directionless scene; reversing the order fixed it. That third one is first-party proof from this exact pipeline, not borrowed theory.

Locations & territories — Camera/geometry first is right for a place: geometry substantially is a location's identity, the thing that must never drift. The existing camera → temporal → infrastructure → material → dual register → population → itemized exclusions → aesthetic-register order stays exactly as is.

Characters — The old order copied the location branch's camera-first sequence onto characters too. Fixed: a character anchor now leads with the concrete physical description that must read the same in every future variant, before camera, temporal frame, wardrobe, environment, expression, and a closing restatement of the hard hands/no-prop/no-staged-moment constraint (kept at the end, where recency already reinforces it correctly). All 35 stored anchors were reordered — word-for-word verified, nothing added or reworded, with the build clean. Still not confirmed against a real Nano Banana Pro render — that needs the author to run one and report back.

Alterations — Alteration (Ⓐ Ⓑ Ⓒ Ⓓ) prompts already front-load invariants correctly; the closing Ⓓ line carries a brief echo of the specific change, so the actual edit content is reinforced at the end too rather than left to stand alone in the middle. Applied to the 12 alterations (7 character, 5 location) that were still pure boilerplate; the other 7 already carried specific closing content and were left untouched.

Locations and territories carry no risk from the reorder, since nothing about their order changed. The character order is a reasoned position from the evidence above and this project's own African Belt pilot; it has not been separately re-piloted since.

Leonardo.ai — Nano Banana Pro generation settings

Recommended settings for generating character portraits and location plates through Leonardo.ai using the Nano Banana Pro model. Reasoned from Leonardo/Google documentation plus this project's own established prompt architecture and house rules — not generic defaults. Re-check if Leonardo materially changes this settings panel. Style corrected 2026-08-17 — see the "Prompt ordering discipline" entry below for why.

Prompt Enhance
Off
Style
Pro color photography (or Pro film photography)
Ratio — characters
1:1
Ratio — locations and territory establishing shots
16:9
Size
Medium — 2752×1536
Why these settings

Prompt Enhance — Guarantees the exact, audit-passed prompt text reaches the generator unmodified. The variant system fingerprints "the prompt the image was actually made from" to detect staleness — a silent Leonardo rewrite would break that match. Auto’s own rule ("long prompts will not be modified") should be a no-op on our 280–414-word anchors anyway, but Off is the only setting that guarantees it, including for shorter edit prompts.

Style — Use the Pro photography preset, never Dynamic. Dynamic reads glossy/hyper-saturated/illustrative — it actively fights the "photorealistic film still, grounded documentary realism" language every prompt already states. None doesn't fight that language, but doesn't reinforce it either, leaving the model's own default tendency unconstrained on the photorealism axis. Pro color/film photography reinforces the exact register the prompt text already commits to. See the "Prompt ordering discipline" reference entry below for the full reasoning trail.

Ratio — characters — Locked house rule: all character plates are strict 1:1 square (World, character portrait standards).

Ratio — locations and territory establishing shots — Locked house rule: all location plates are strict 16:9 cinematic landscape (World). Territory establishing shots use the same 16:9 environmental frame, not the 1:1 portrait frame (Image Canon).

Size — Matches Google’s documented "2K" tier for Nano Banana Pro — the real quality knee (sharper edges/detail than 1K/Small) versus Large (~"4K"), whose extra pixels mainly help large-format print or heavy zoom, not a browser. The site never displays anywhere near 2752px wide, so Large buys no visible quality on-site — Medium isn’t just the affordable choice, it’s the objectively correct one for this use case.

For alterations (v2+): select Medium explicitly on every generation in an arc, don’t assume it inherits the anchor’s size — the Leonardo web UI doesn’t expose the API’s "match input resolution" behavior, so an explicit, consistent size keeps a subject’s variants pixel-dimension-consistent with each other. 2:3 ratio is available but unused — nothing in current canon needs a portrait-oriented frame.

Sources: docs.leonardo.ai/docs/nano-banana-pro; replicate.com/blog/how-to-prompt-nano-banana-pro; help.apiyi.com (Nano Banana Pro 1K/2K/4K resolution guide)
