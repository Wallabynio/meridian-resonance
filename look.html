07 — Look Book

How the world of Meridian Resonance looks in pictures. The Look Book turns the Story Bible into images: every image the project uses, on the site, in the design system or as reference while drafting, is made from an entry here. When the Look Book and the other Story Bible documents disagree, they win and the Look Book is corrected.

07 — Look Book · 1

Rules

  1. Canon first. An image shows the world as 05 - World.md and 06 - Daily Life.md describe it: the white sky, air-carbon towers, the thread on the neck, the radiators on the ships. Nothing in a picture is invented that the documents don't hold, and anything new a picture needs goes through the Story Bible first.
  2. Photographs, not paintings. Every image looks like a still from a documentary or a film shot on location in 2095: real lenses, real light, real weather, real wear. No illustration, no concept-art gloss, no 3D-render sheen.
  3. The future is ordinary. Nobody in a picture is amazed by the world. Technology is used, worn, dirty and maintained. The wonder is in scale and in light, not in gadgets.
  4. Two charges at once. Each image carries beauty and dread together (the design system's rule): a lovely sunset that is copper because of the sprayers; a warm Haven corridor with the air budget on the wall.
  5. One source of light. Dark, wide frames lit from one direction, with room at the bottom left for the site's text scrim (the design system's imagery rules).
  6. No text in the image. Signs, labels and screens are blurred, angled away or out of frame, so nothing reads as gibberish and nothing dates.
  7. No real brands, no real people, no recognisable living faces.

07 — Look Book · 2

The camera

PlacesWide: 24–35 mm equivalent, eye level or a little above, the horizon low. For scale, a long lens from a distance (the wall from the Rockaways, a ship's radiators from orbit)
People50–85 mm, waist up or closer, in their own place, looking near the camera, never posed against a backdrop
ThingsIn use, in context, with a hand or a body for scale. Never a product shot on a plain ground
Grain and gradeFine film grain; a restrained grade; deep, true blacks; no bloom, no lens flare
Format16:9 at 2400 px for every library image; 21:9 crops for chapter strips; each portrait also cut to a 480 px square face for avatars (design system)

07 — Look Book · 5

People

  • The world's faces. Every ethnicity and age, weighted the way 2095's migrations weighted them. Skin weathered by heat; squints against the white sky; sunburn and heat rash are ordinary.
  • The thread. Linked people show a hair-thin trace down the side of the neck, never glowing. The link-capable have a small capped socket behind the ear.
  • The dark towns. Handhelds on cords around the neck; no threads, or threads switched off and covered.
  • Dress. As 06 - Daily Life.md, section 8: pale cool-weave, hats and veils by day; Charter black with open necks; Mandate high collars; civic crews in reflective gear with names across the back; Compact clothes visibly mended; Haven's close earth-coloured layers; station coveralls with crossing patches.
  • Off Earth. Haven-born people are tall and fine-boned. Spacers carry a dosimeter on the wrist.
  • Synthetic persons. A chassis in the room like any other person: same eye line, same framing, never a robot on display.

07 — Look Book · 6

Technology on camera

  • The overlay is on the eye, so it never floats in the air. At most, one point-of-view shot shows a faint overlay across a real view. No holograms anywhere.
  • Interfaces are worn, not shown off. The lace is a detail, not a light.
  • Machines are matte and maintained. Scuffs, labels (blurred), repair tape in the dark towns.
  • Ships are mostly radiator. The red wings dominate any ship seen from outside.
  • The white sky is in every outdoor Earth image.

07 — Look Book · 7

Never

Blue skies over Earth; snow in a New York January (it rains); flying cars; chrome; floating screens; neon cyberpunk; retro rocket shapes; purple nebulae; lens flares; glowing circuits on skin; clean, untouched Mars; a breathable-looking Mars; Mars with Earth-blue daylight; crowds of identical people; heroic poses; anything legible.

07 — Look Book · 8

Making the set consistent

One model, one style blockAll images from one model and one shared style block (below), appended to every prompt
CharactersEach character's portrait becomes a reusable reference, so the same face appears in every image they're in
PlacesEach major place's first approved image becomes the reference for its other shots
ReviewEvery image is checked against its entry before it enters the library; anything that breaks a rule above is redone, not kept

The style block (appended to every prompt):

Photorealistic documentary film still, shot on location in the year 2095, natural single-source light, fine film grain, restrained colour grade, deep blacks, no text, no logos, no lens flare, no holograms, nothing glossy or rendered.

Plus, for Earth exteriors: "pale milky white sky, hazy and nearly shadowless." For Mars exteriors: "butterscotch Martian sky, fine red dust in the air." For Haven interiors: "warm honey light from living walls."

07 — Look Book · 10

Naming and the library

Each image gets a lowercase slug named for what it shows (new-york-from-the-sea, haven-commons), kept in the design system in groups: Heroes, Earth, Orbit and the Moon, Mars, Between worlds, Characters, People of the age, Things, Daily life. Each group's notes say which Look Book entry every image fulfils. Every library image must fulfil an entry; images made for earlier versions of the world are kept in Archive/Images/, not in the library.

Each entry gives the image's slug, what it must show, what it must avoid, where it is used, and its prompt.
